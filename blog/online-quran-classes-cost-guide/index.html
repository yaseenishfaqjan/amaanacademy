If you are comparing online Quran academies, the first question is almost always the same: how much will it cost? It is a fair and important question, and you deserve an honest answer. The truth is that there is no single "sticker price" that fits every learner, because a good Quran program is built around the student, not the other way around. What we can do here is explain exactly what drives the cost up or down, what you should genuinely be paying for, and how to get an exact, no-pressure quote for your own family.

What affects the cost of online Quran classes?

Across the market, the fee for online Quran classes is shaped by a handful of clear factors. Once you understand them, pricing stops feeling mysterious and you can judge whether an academy is offering real value or simply the cheapest possible option. The main things that move the price are:

  • How many classes you take per week (frequency is the single biggest factor).
  • The length of each session, often 30 minutes for younger children or 45–60 minutes for older students and adults.
  • The program you choose — basic Qaida and reading, Nazra, Tajweed, or full Hifz.
  • Whether lessons are strictly one-on-one or shared in a group.
  • The qualification and experience of the teacher, such as a certified Aalim or Hafiz.
  • Your region and currency, since families in the USA, UK, Canada, Australia and the UAE all see slightly different norms.

None of these factors work in isolation. A student doing two short group reading sessions a week sits at one end of the range, while a learner doing five one-on-one Hifz lessons with a senior Hafiz sits at the other. Everything in between is a matter of matching the plan to the goal.

How does the number of classes per week change the price?

Most reputable academies price by the number of classes per week, usually billed monthly. A student attending twice a week will naturally pay less than one attending four or five times a week, simply because they are receiving less teaching time. This is good news, because it means you are not locked into a one-size-fits-all package. You choose the pace that suits your schedule, your budget and your learning goal, and the fee follows from that.

As a rough guide to planning: two or three classes a week is comfortable for steady reading and Tajweed progress, while four or five classes a week suits anyone with a serious memorisation goal or a child who thrives on daily routine. More frequent classes cost more in total, but they often deliver faster, more lasting results — which can make them better value per month of actual progress, not just per lesson.

Does the type of program, like Hifz, affect the fee?

Yes. Different programs ask different things of both the student and the teacher, and that is reflected in how plans are built. Beginner reading with Noorani Qaida, Nazra recitation and Tajweed refinement are each structured programs with their own pace. A full Hifz (memorisation) program is the most intensive of all: it typically needs more frequent sessions, careful daily revision of previously memorised portions, and a Hafiz teacher who can listen attentively and correct with precision.

Because Hifz demands more class time and more specialised teaching, it generally sits at the higher end of any academy's range — not because the academy is charging a premium for the sake of it, but because memorising the Book of Allah properly is a serious undertaking that deserves serious support. You can explore the full range of courses on our programs page to see which path matches your intention.

Is one-on-one more expensive than group classes, and is it worth it?

One-on-one classes usually cost more than group classes, and this is where many families face their real decision. A very cheap group class can look attractive at first glance, but it is worth understanding what you are actually buying. In a group, one teacher divides their attention among several students. A shy child may stay quiet, a struggling reader may be carried along without truly understanding, and individual mistakes in pronunciation or Tajweed can go uncorrected for weeks.

In a dedicated one-on-one class, every minute belongs to your child or to you. The teacher hears every letter, corrects every error immediately, and moves at exactly the right pace — slowing down where it is hard and speeding up where it is easy. For something as precious and precise as Quran recitation, where a single mispronounced letter changes the meaning, that focused attention is not a luxury. It is the point. This is why we believe a well-taught one-on-one lesson is worth far more than several cheap, crowded group sessions, even though the headline price is higher.

How much does the teacher's qualification and experience matter?

A great deal. The person teaching your family the Quran is the most important part of the whole arrangement, and their training is a legitimate reason for differences in price. A certified Aalim or Hafiz with years of teaching experience, strong Tajweed, and the patience to work with children and nervous adults is simply not the same as an untrained tutor offering the lowest rate they can.

At Amaan Academy every teacher is a qualified Aalim or Hafiz who can teach comfortably in both English and Urdu, so lessons are clear whether you are in London, Toronto, Sydney, Dubai or anywhere in the USA. When you weigh up fees, remember that you are not buying minutes on a screen — you are entrusting your family's relationship with the Quran to a teacher. Quality teaching is the one place it is never wise to cut corners.
